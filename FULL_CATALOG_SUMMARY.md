# Five-Millennium-style Eclipse Catalog — Full DE441 Span

**Coverage:** years **−13198 to +17189** (30,388 years). Astronomical year numbering
(year 0 = 1 BCE); Julian calendar before 1582 Oct 15, Gregorian after. The first year
is partial: the scan starts at Gregorian −13198 Jan 1 = Julian −13198 Apr 12.
**Total eclipses:** **145,356** — 72,224 solar, 73,132 lunar (~4.78 / yr).
**Ephemeris:** JPL **DE441** (covers −13200 Aug 15 .. 17191 Mar 15). Chunk 6 stops at
17189, ~2 years inside the edge, leaving margin for the syzygy search.

## Deliverables
- `Outputs/eclipse_catalog_full_-13198_17189_detailed.csv` — Date, TD, ΔT, kind, type code, type,
  Gamma, magnitude, umbral & penumbral magnitudes, plus Forward and Reverse Saros Count
  (`~` = estimated for series cut off by the catalog edge).
- `eclipse_catalog_full_formatted.csv` (not included; regenerated by the pipeline) — analysis file (adds Year/Month/Day, cum. seconds,
  Saros numbers and positions).
- (The 6-column stitched CSV and the six per-chunk CSVs were deleted to save space; they
  are regenerable.)

## Method
- Syzygies located directly from the ephemeris (not analytic series, which degrade far
  from J2000), then the instant of greatest eclipse refined numerically (shadow-axis
  closest approach for solar; antisolar axis for lunar).
- Apparent geocentric positions (light-time + aberration + deflection).
- NASA-matched calibrations: lunar shadow +63.5 km atmospheric shell; solar-magnitude
  Moon radius 1736.81 km (k = 0.272307).
- ΔT = Espenak & Meeus 2006 + NASA's lunar secular-acceleration correction
  −0.000012932·(y−1955)² (outside 1955–2005). Revised 2026-10-20; see README change log.

## Comparison with other catalogs
- **1000–2000 vs NASA SEcat5/LEcat5:** reproduced exactly (0 missing; TD ~0.5 s;
  γ ~5×10⁻⁵; magnitude ~1.5×10⁻⁴). Re-run 2026-10-20 on the final full catalog: same.
- **−3999 to +4000 vs Yuk Tung Liu's catalog** (independent ELP/MPP02 + VSOP pipeline,
  https://ytliu.epizy.com/eclipse/), across the full overlap:
  - Identical eclipse counts; 100% matched by date (one zero-magnitude grazing partial
    "traded" in the far future).
  - Solar TD ≤ 1 s; lunar TD ≤ 13 s (median 1–2 s); gamma identical to 4 decimals;
    magnitudes agree to ~1×10⁻⁴ (solar) / ~3×10⁻⁴ (lunar).
  - Type agreement ≥ 99.8%; all differences sit at definitional boundaries
    (total↔hybrid, hybrid↔annular, penumbral↔partial, central↔non-central annular),
    where the deciding quantity matches to ≤ 0.0003.
- **Saros–Inex panorama by Luca Quaglia & John Tilley** (computed with the Solex numerical
  integrator; hosted on NASA's eclipse website; −11000..+15000): 61,231 of 61,775 solar
  cells at identical Saros/Inex coordinates.
- **Saros numbering:** inside −3999..+4000, Matt Slavik's own numbering of an eclipse list
  built from Yuk Tung Liu's catalogs (`_saros_numbers.xlsx`) — matched to NASA's Saros
  numbering wherever it could be verified, calculated where it could not; outside that
  range, this catalog's own mod-223 extension (see README "Known limitations" and
  pipeline/apply_saros.py).

## Accuracy notes / caveats
- **Range covered by external comparisons: −3999 to +4000** (Liu), including 1001–2000 (NASA).
- **Outside that** — deep BCE (−13198..−4000) and far future (+4001..+17189) — there is
  no external catalog; checks there are internal only (eclipse frequency, solar/lunar
  balance, no missing interior Saros members, valid dates). Eclipse **times** remain
  DE441-grade across the whole span; only **ΔT** (the Earth-rotation clock) is
  model-extrapolated and thus uncertain far from the present — ΔT does not affect the
  TD-of-greatest-eclipse column.

## Reproduce
- One chunk:  `python eclipse_catalog.py START END`   (needs de441.bsp in the folder)
- Stitch:     `python pipeline/stitch.py`
- Structural check:  `python pipeline/sanity_check.py [csv y0 y1]...`
- Cross-check vs Liu: `python pipeline/compare_ref.py detailed.csv y0 y1`
